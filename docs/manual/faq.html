TruckSim Dispatcher · FAQ
The short version

What is this, actually?

American Truck Simulator hands you a truck and a job list and lets you do whatever you like. This app puts a carrier on the other end of the radio.

You are a company driver. You report where you are and what your clocks say; dispatch tells you which load you are running and why. You close the load out; the company audits it, pays you on Friday, and writes up your file. Miss enough windows and there is a conversation. Wreck enough equipment and there is a different one.

Nothing is enforced. The app cannot stop you doing anything in ATS — it is a second screen that treats your game as a job.

The point is the pressure, not the paperwork

Freight in ATS pays whether you take the good load or the bad one. Here somebody is watching the rate, the empty miles, your hours, your home time and the state of the truck — and they say no. A run you would have taken without thinking gets refused with a number attached.

What it is not

Not a modNothing is installed into ATS. No files touched, no launch options, no risk to your profile.
Not telemetryIt does not read the game. There is no plugin, no memory reading, no SDK. You tell it what the screen says.
Not a money cheatIt pays you, the driver, on its own books. What ATS does with company money is between you and ATS.
Not multiplayerSingle career, on your machine. TruckersMP is not supported and would not know about it.

Is it for you?

Probably, if You already roleplay a bit — running to hours, keeping a schedule, refusing the silly loads. This gives that structure and an opinion to argue with.
Probably not, if You play ATS to unwind and do not want a second screen asking you for numbers. It is deliberately more work than the game is on its own.

The company is not on your side

It is not a helper that finds you good loads. It has its own interests — margin, utilisation, the state of its equipment — and sometimes those are yours and sometimes they are not. It will send you somewhere thin because the rate was good, and tell you afterwards that the reload is your problem. That is the game.

Getting going

What do I need, and how do I start?

To run it: nothing but the exe

Download the zip, unpack it anywhere, double-click TruckSimDispatcher.exe. A console window opens and your browser opens with it. Leave the console open while you play; Ctrl+C shuts it down. No install, no .NET runtime, no account, no internet.

Your career lives in a data folder beside the exe. Back it up by copying that folder — or use Settings → Data, which will take one for you.

Put it on a second monitor

It is a browser page. Alt-tab works, a phone or tablet on the same network works, and a second screen beside the game is what it was built for. You will be typing into it between legs, not while driving.

One thing you will need in ATS itself

The developer console, for rests and restarts

This app runs you to hours-of-service rules, and ATS has no way to sit still for ten hours, let alone thirty-four. The game's own sleep advances a fixed amount at a rest area and no more, so a 10-hour reset or a 34-hour restart cannot be taken in the game as it ships.

Enable the developer console and advance the clock by the hours the app tells you to sit. It is a game limitation, not a choice the app made — and without it the whole HOS side of this is unplayable, because you will never legally get your hours back.

Turning it on is a one-line change to config.cfg in your ATS profile folder, and it is a standard thing to run with. Nothing else here needs it.

Genuinely optional, and what each is for

A save editor Occasionally useful, never required. Two reasons come up. Seeding starting cash if you want to begin bigger than a one-truck outfit. And putting money back when the app's books hold more than ATS does — a service the company paid for on a hired driver's tractor, say, which the app booked and the game knows nothing about. It tells you the exact figure. TS SE Tool with SII_Decrypt is the usual pairing. Back the save up first: editors can corrupt one and SCS cannot support a modified profile.
An API key Only if you want to paste screenshots. Without one you type the numbers, which is perfectly normal and nothing is lost. With one you can paste the job board or your HOS screen and it reads the rows out. It is an Anthropic key from console.anthropic.com — a developer key, not a Claude subscription login — and you pay Anthropic for what you use.

How a session goes

What does it actually feel like to use?

Four things, and only the first two happen every leg.

1Report in. Where you are, the game clock, your four HOS clocks, fuel, damage. Type it or paste a screenshot.
2Show the board. The handful of jobs going out from where you are parked first; the whole city only if none of those work. Dispatch picks one and shows its working — every term, with its contribution to the score.
3Run it in ATS. The app does nothing while you drive. Fuel stops and breakdowns get logged if they happen.
4Close it out. Miles, times, damage, costs. You get an audit: what it earned, what it cost, whether it was on time and whose fault it was if not.

You do not pick your own freight

Dispatch chooses. You can ask for something else and it will answer, and off probation you get more latitude — but a company driver does not shop the board, and that is the whole arrangement. If you want to overrule it you can, and it is recorded as your call.

Where the time goes

Entering a board is the bulk of it — a handful of jobs, four fields each that matter. Everything else is a few numbers off the game screen. Pasting screenshots collapses most of that if you have a key set.

It will refuse loads, and it will be annoying

That is the feature. A load that cannot make its window is refused with the arithmetic attached. A dock offering nothing worth the truck gets held while it asks for the city board. Being told "no, and here is why" is the thing ATS never does.

Things it will interrupt you about

Home timeYou pick an interval at hire — weekly through to six weeks. As the date closes, freight that runs the wrong way starts losing arguments, and once it is overdue they stop being arguments.
Your hoursRunning low on the 70 puts a restart on the plan before you are stuck, and it checks whether recap gets you there cheaper first.
The truckA service coming due, damage worth fixing, or a unit at the end of its life — with what to do about it in ATS.

What the company watches

Safety, equipment and what lands on your file

Incidents are graded by what they did

You report what happened and how much damage it caused; Safety decides the rest. A 2% kerbing and a 26% rollover are not the same event, and they do not walk the same ladder:

CoachingA conversation. Where a first small preventable lands.
Written warningOn the file properly.
Final warningWhere a wreck starts, rather than where it builds up to.
Downgrade · SuspensionThe truck, then the seat.
TerminationA second-chance carrier will look past it. That is what they are for.

Clean work walks it off: a minor event stops counting against hiring after about twenty loads, a wreck after sixty. It stays on the record permanently — it just stops being a bar.

Only what was your fault counts against you

A shipper who loaded you four hours late, a receiver who would not take you, an AI that turned across you — noted, and not held against you. Three driver-fault late deliveries inside your last ten loads is where lateness starts to bite, so a bad fortnight followed by clean work reads as a driver who improved.

Equipment

Your tractor accumulates wear and needs servicing on a schedule; the yard does the work and the company pays. Damage is reported by you off the game's own figure. A truck that reaches the end of its life is condemned and replaced — you are told what to buy in ATS and to what spec.

Pay, on Friday

Per mileLoaded at your rate, empty at a lower one, plus premiums for reefer, hazmat and oversize.
AccessorialsDetention past the first two hours, layover, breakdown, extra stops, tarps.
BonusesOn-time service, a clean safety period, fuel economy against your truck's rating, and a share of what you save buying fuel well.
DeductionsIt is a W2 stub: medical, federal, Social Security, Medicare and state.

Getting on, and getting out

Probation, promotion and applying elsewhere

You are hired onto a probationary period, and everything after it is earned rather than chosen.

Probation is a period, not a checklist

Ninety days for most hires, reviewed at your first home time after it ends. A strong record can shorten it; a specialised carrier — heavy haul, tanker, car haul — will not go below sixty however tidy your numbers are, because permits and securement take as long as they take.

You report in every fortnight while it runs and get a written review each time. Those are feedback, not the gate. Preventable incidents during it cost you days, and enough of them end it.

The ladder

Probationary → company → senior → lead, then Specialist Driver for the awkward freight and Master Driver at the top. Every rung is applied by the company when the record earns it. There is nothing to click and no rate to set — a driver does not sign off their own promotion any more than they authorise their own equipment.

How far you can go depends on the carrier. Some stop at company driver, and the job market shows what each one tops out at before you take the job.

Applying somewhere better

Resign and apply from the carrier market. Carriers check years behind the wheel, verifiable loads, your service record, average damage, fault incidents, hazmat classes and the skills ATS levels for you. They also have quarters: one having a bad month may not be hiring at all, however good you look.

Time on their kind of freight is a credit, never a bar — needing flatbed time to get a flatbed job is a career that cannot be started. It breaks ties, and the card tells you where you stand before you resign from anything.

Your record travels. The equipment does not.

Loads, miles, on-time percentage and fault incidents follow you between employers, along with the split of what freight you actually hauled. Your unpaid wages are settled before the move completes, so you never leave money on a previous employer's books.

The trucks and trailers belong to the carrier you are leaving. What you own in ATS is still sitting in your garage, so changing jobs comes with a list of what to square up in the game — and the app holds dispatch until it is done rather than sending you out on somebody else's equipment.

Common questions

The ones that come up most

Does it work with my mods? Yes, and it does not need to know about them. You type the city names, so map mods, ProMods, C2C and the rest all work. About thirty thousand real US city coordinates ship with it, so a town the app's own market table has never heard of still gets a real distance.
Which DLC do I need? None in particular. It follows wherever you drive.
Can I cheat it? Trivially — you are the only source of numbers. The app is built so that cheating is boring rather than impossible: every judgement is seeded, so reloading does not re-roll an answer you did not like, and the audit tells you when a figure does not add up.
Does it use real HOS rules? The US FMCSA pattern: 11 driving, 14 on duty, a 30-minute break, 70 hours in 8 days, a 10-hour reset and a 34-hour restart. Modelled for a game. Do not use it to plan real driving.
Is my save safe? The app never opens it. The only risk in this whole workflow is a save editor, if you choose to use one.
Where does my data go? Nowhere. Everything is on your machine, no accounts and no network calls — unless you set an API key for screenshots, which is off by default and clearly marked where it is not.
Can I run my own fleet? Yes. Hire drivers in ATS, add them on the Fleet tab, and file a periodic fleet report. The company handles their maintenance, replaces worn-out tractors and occasionally loses one of them to a competitor.
Do I have to start at the bottom? You start on probation somewhere that will take you. Where that is depends on the experience you declare, and carriers check it against your record as you build one.
What if I get fired? A second-chance carrier takes you, on a shorter leash and a worse rate. Being let go by one of those is where a career actually ends.
Can I change what I haul? Ask operations to re-rig you onto a different trailer. It is a request, answered at your next close-out, and it can be turned down. Drop and hook — no trailer of your own, the shipper's box every time — is its own arrangement, and the dedicated version of it is the best seat in the fleet.
How long is a session? As long as you want. It saves continuously, and picking up mid-load is normal — the active trip and your clocks are where you left them.
Does it need to be running while I drive? No. Close it and reopen it later. It only ever knows what you have told it.

If something looks wrong

Reading it, and reporting it

Two manuals ship with this

User Manual What to do, in order. Read the first few sections and you can play; the rest is reference.
Operations Manual Why every rule exists — each threshold, each mechanism, and the failure it was built to prevent. You never need it to play. It is numbered against the User Manual, so a page headed From Section 13 belongs behind Section 13 there.

Almost every rule came from somebody playing

The reason the Operations Manual reads like a list of mistakes is that it is one. Loads that were refused for the wrong reason, hours credited that did not exist, a driver failed for a shipper's delay — each of those was somebody reporting it from a real career, and the fix is written up beside the fault.

So a report of "this feels wrong" is worth more than it sounds, even without a diagnosis attached.

What helps when reporting

  • The build stamp from the app header — the version alone is not enough, since a dozen builds can carry the same number.
  • What you expected, and what happened instead.
  • The scoring detail on the load card, if it is a dispatch decision you disagree with. Every term is itemised, so the disagreement can usually be traced to one number.
  • Your career.json, if you are willing. It settles in one look what guessing does not.

Two things that are almost always the answer

"It picked the wrong load." Open the scoring detail. Score ranks the candidates, but the hard gates decide who is a candidate at all — so a higher number can sit on a card that was not taken, and the app now says which one and why.

"It has not noticed something." It only knows what you have told it. A clock you have not reported, a board you have not entered, an incident you have not filed: none of it exists. The app never reads your game and never guesses at a figure.

It is alpha, and that is not a disclaimer

Play-testing feedback is the reason it is in your hands at all. Keep backups of your career file — Settings → Data will take one — and expect things to move.
